# Feature 2: Style Transfer Matrix

## What It Does

Applies the visual language (lighting, color science, texture emphasis, composition grammar) of one category to the subject matter of another.

Unlike the Mixer (which combines two specific prompts), Style Transfer takes the *entire aesthetic system* of a category and applies it to a subject from elsewhere.

## The Matrix

| Apply Style Of → | To Subject Of ↓ | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Film Noir | Portrait | Hard shadow, high contrast character study |
| Underwater | Fashion | Fabric-in-water physics, blue-shift color |
| Vintage 1950s | Food | Kodachrome color science, period styling |
| Night Neon | Product | Mixed-color reflections on surfaces |
| Surreal | Architecture | Impossible buildings, real materials |
| Nature Wildlife | Portrait | Telephoto compression, natural environment |
| Street Photography | Fashion | Documentary candid, available light |
| Cinematic Sci-Fi | Product | Futuristic context, practical LED lighting |
| Boudoir Artistic | Landscape | Body-as-landscape abstraction |
| Travel Landscape | Food | Location-specific cuisine in environment |

## How to Apply

### Step 1: Identify the Style DNA

Each category has a distinct visual DNA:

| Category | Core DNA |
|---|---|
| **Portrait** | Single-subject focus, catchlight, skin texture, expression reading |
| **Fashion** | Garment-first, fabric physics, editorial pose, brand narrative |
| **Cinematic** | Narrative moment, anamorphic, motivated lighting, film grammar |
| **Product** | Hero angle, material interaction, controlled environment |
| **Night Neon** | Mixed color temp, wet reflections, high ISO grain, practical sources |
| **Vintage** | Period film stock, era styling, grain structure, color science |
| **Street** | Decisive moment, available light, environmental storytelling |
| **Nature** | Telephoto compression, behavioral moment, patience, natural light |
| **Surreal** | Impossible + physically grounded, real materials on absurd forms |
| **Underwater** | Blue-shift, particle suspension, fabric/hair water behavior |

### Step 2: Apply the Formula

```
STYLE TRANSFER = [Subject/Content of Category A]
                + [Lighting Logic of Category B]
                + [Color Science of Category B]
                + [Composition Grammar of Category B]
                + [Texture Emphasis of Category B]
                + [Technical Look of Category B] (grain, lens, format)
```

### Step 3: Resolve Conflicts

The subject determines WHAT is in frame.
The style determines HOW it looks.

When they conflict:
- Subject wardrobe adjusts to style (period accuracy for vintage, waterproofing for underwater)
- Environment adjusts to style (noir needs urban night, even if subject is from nature)
- Lighting is 100% from style — never mix lighting approaches

## Example Transfers

### Film Noir Style → Food Subject
> A single whiskey glass and a half-eaten steak on a diner counter, lit by a single harsh overhead lamp creating a tight pool surrounded by black void. Venetian blind shadows stripe the plate. The steak shows proper Maillard crust with fat catching specular. Smoke from an unseen cigarette creates volumetric atmosphere. Shot in monochromatic warm tones — near-black and white with sepia warmth. The diner counter shows decades of wear. 50mm anamorphic, locked-off, Storaro noir palette.

### Underwater Style → Fashion Editorial Subject
> A haute couture structured wool coat with geometric shoulders submerged in clear blue water, suspended on an invisible model form, the heavy wool fabric behaving completely differently underwater — unable to drape, instead floating with buoyancy creating alien silhouettes. Individual wool fibers separate in the water. Caustic light patterns play across the dark fabric surface from above. Fine silt particles drift past. The structured shoulders maintain their shape due to internal boning while the hem floats upward. 16mm dome port, available light from above.

### Vintage 1970s Style → Portrait Subject
> A 34-year-old Black female executive in structured navy blazer — but rendered in pushed Kodak Portra 400 with warm color cast, visible grain structure, and the slightly desaturated shadow tones of a 1970s portrait sitting. The subject is contemporary but the photographic treatment is period: slightly soft focus from slower vintage glass, halation around bright edges, and the particular skin-tone rendering of that era's emulsion chemistry. Background shows warm amber tones of period-style tungsten lighting through a sheer curtain. 85mm f/2 vintage Nikkor.
